# What Geothority Actually Does for the End User

This page is written as a marketing-ready product deep dive. It is grounded in the actual app, but phrased for a real buyer, operator, or future email reader.

---

## Big picture
Geothority is a local search operations platform for service businesses, especially insurance agents. It is designed to do three things for the user:

1. **Diagnose** local visibility problems quickly
2. **Prioritize** the fixes that will matter most
3. **Help the user get those fixes handled from one system**

The core promise is not just "here is data." The actual experience is closer to: **scan my local presence, show me what is broken, and help me act on it.**

---

## What the end user can do inside Geothority

## 1. Understand their local SEO position quickly
A user can enter their business website, business name, city, and state, then run a scan.

From that scan, the app evaluates the business across Google-facing trust signals, directory consistency, content depth, review presence, and AI search readiness.

For the end user, this means they can:
- see whether their local visibility is healthy or weak
- stop guessing why a competitor is outranking them
- get a single score plus breakdowns instead of a confusing SEO spreadsheet
- understand what matters first, second, and third

This is one of the strongest parts of the product because it gives the user a fast, understandable starting point.

---

## 2. Get a Trust Stack score instead of random SEO advice
Geothority organizes its diagnosis into a 5-layer Trust Stack.

For the user, that means the app breaks their local presence into:
- **Foundation**
- **Trust Pages**
- **Geo Content**
- **Reviews**
- **AI Optimization**

Instead of just saying "your SEO needs work," it shows:
- which layer is strongest
- which layer is weakest
- which improvements would likely move the score most
- how those layers compare over time

This is important because it turns local SEO into something operational and understandable.

---

## 3. See exactly what the scanner checks
The scan is not a vague black box. It checks many specific things that matter to local discovery.

### Foundation checks
- SSL presence and validity
- page load basics
- robots.txt
- sitemap.xml
- H1 presence
- viewport tags
- Open Graph tags
- Twitter Card tags
- business identity signals like name, address, and phone consistency
- Google Business Profile link presence

### Trust page checks
- About page presence
- FAQ page presence
- licensing and credential language
- service area pages

### Geo content checks
- city-specific pages
- internal link signals
- local relevance depth

### Review checks
- review link presence
- review mentions on site
- review health indicators

### AI optimization checks
- schema presence
- FAQ schema
- LocalBusiness schema
- entity and structured-data signals that can influence AI answers

For the user, this means the system can explain not only that something is wrong, but what kind of thing is wrong.

---

## 4. Get actionable quick wins, not just reports
After a scan, the user gets a result page with concrete recommendations.

That page can show:
- the overall Trust Stack score
- per-layer scores
- quick-win cards
- competitor gaps
- a fix package

The quick wins are useful because they move the app from analysis into action. The user can understand:
- what to fix
- how serious it is
- what likely impact it may have

This matters because most local SEO tools stop at diagnosis. Geothority tries to bridge diagnosis and remediation.

---

## 5. Generate a "fix package" from scan results
One of the most valuable end-user experiences is the ability to trigger a packaged set of recommended fixes.

The app can generate:
- schema markup
- FAQ content
- About page content
- landing page content
- meta tags
- listing sync instructions
- AI optimization recommendations

For the user, this means they do not just learn they have missing trust or structure. They can get draft assets that help close those gaps faster.

This is a major product strength because it reduces the distance between insight and implementation.

---

## 6. Export scan findings into a PDF report
A user can export scan findings into a PDF-style report.

That helps with:
- internal review
- client review if used in an agency-style workflow
- keeping a record of before/after performance
- sharing findings with a developer, marketer, or business partner

This helps make the output portable instead of trapped inside the dashboard.

---

## 7. See scan history and performance over time
Geothority stores past scans and exposes them in multiple places.

A user can:
- view recent scans
- open prior scan detail pages
- compare changes over time
- review trend direction
- use reports/history to track progress

For the end user, this turns Geothority from a one-time checker into a system for ongoing visibility management.

---

## 8. Use a dashboard as an operating surface, not just a landing page after login
Inside the app, the dashboard is designed to summarize the business state.

A user can use the dashboard to:
- view the latest scan summary
- see top quick wins
- review score history
- see recent scans
- spot current visibility movement at a glance
- get prompted toward the next action if they are new

For the user, the dashboard answers: "Where do I stand right now?"

## 9. Generate local landing pages and SEO content
The app includes a content generation workflow.

A user can generate:
- local landing pages
- service-specific content
- city-focused pages
- trust-supporting page drafts

The generation flow supports:
- city input
- service selection
- business name
- agent name
- live generation feedback
- previewing the result

For the user, this means Geothority can become a content production tool, not just a scanner.

This is particularly valuable for businesses that know they need city pages but do not want to write them manually.

The product does not stop at generating one page. It includes a content area where the user can:

- review drafts
- see status

That makes content generation feel like part of a workflow instead of a one-off feature.

## 11. Publish content to a connected CMS
There is publishing support for connected CMS workflows.

For the user, this means generated content can move from draft into publication more quickly if their CMS setup is present.

This matters because it reduces copy-paste friction and makes the app more operationally useful.

## 12. Audit competitors and benchmark against them
The app has a competitor area intended to show how the user stacks up against nearby competitors.

For the user, that means the platform can help answer questions like:
- who is beating me locally
- where are they stronger
- how far behind or ahead am I
- where should I focus to close the gap

This is valuable positioning-wise because buyers want more than self-analysis. They want market context.

Important note: some competitor surfaces appear partially mocked today, so this is a strong concept with some current implementation limits.

## 13. Monitor Google Business Profile performance and health
Geothority includes a GBP area and monitoring surfaces.

For the user, that means the platform can help with:
- checking GBP status
- monitoring profile-related data
- tracking ongoing changes
- surfacing weekly monitoring workflows

This is strategically important because Google Business Profile health strongly influences local ranking and map visibility.

Some of the deeper live data depends on Google authentication and full GBP connection state.

## 14. Audit citations and listing consistency
The app includes citation audit functionality.

For the end user, that means they can understand whether their business identity is consistent across directories and local data sources.

This matters because inconsistent business data can quietly weaken rankings and trust.

The end-user value here is:
- clearer visibility into listings problems
- a path toward cleanup
- more confidence that core business identity signals align across platforms

## 15. Check AI search visibility
One of Geothority's strongest differentiators is that it does not stop at classic local SEO.

The app also checks whether the business is visible in AI-driven answer environments, including references to:
- ChatGPT
- Perplexity
- Google AI Overviews

For the end user, this means they can start preparing not just for search rankings, but for the shift toward AI recommendation surfaces.

This is a serious differentiator because most traditional local SEO tools still focus mostly on classic directories and SERP positions.

This lowers the technical barrier for non-technical business owners.

## What should be framed carefully in marketing
A few things should be marketed honestly and carefully because they are partly dependent on setup, integrations, or deeper implementation work.

These include:
- some competitor-watchdog depth
- some citation-sync scale claims
- some GBP live-data experiences without Google connection
- certain higher-tier promises like review campaigns, weekly GBP posting, white-labeling, agency-grade API access, and service layers that may need more wiring or operational support

The safest marketing position is:
- lead hard with what is real and impressive now
- frame some advanced items as platform capabilities or managed workflows, not as magic buttons unless they are fully live
